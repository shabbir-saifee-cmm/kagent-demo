Demo 3 · Identity

The agent acts for you, never beyond you

An agent can have its own identity for work it does on its own. When it works for you, it uses your access, and what it does carries your name.

01Only what you're allowed to do

Your permissions, limited to the tools we allow the agent. Asking it, or tricking it, never gets it more access than you have.

What's next

What we do next in Lava

Deploying agents is the easy part. What no platform decides for us is identity: who an agent is, who it acts for, and what it may do.

01Start with quick wins

Simple agents that need no complex setup, like Jira hygiene and refinement prep. Each gets an owner and a measure, such as hours saved per sprint.

02Treat skills like code

Versioned and reviewed by PR, then tested and benchmarked against real examples before any agent uses a new version.

03Agent identity, designed with Security

Okta integration, every agent registered with an owner, and clear rules for when it acts as a person or as itself.

04The platform in a real Lava cluster

Sandboxing, the gateway, audit in our existing logging, and cost limits on Foundry, proven in our environment.

Summary

Agents can work while we don't,
because the platform holds the line

Schedules start them. Guardrails contain them. Identity limits them to the person they work for.
